# Fact check: The 4-Hour Battle That Made Every Navy Obsolete

Checked on 2026-11-15 against the sources listed at the bottom. Scene numbers count from 0, as in
`hampton-roads.json`. "Primary" means the wording was read in the source text itself; "secondary" means it
was confirmed only through a search summary of that page, so open the link before relying on it.

## Still open: check these before publishing

| Scene | Claim | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Greene is "barely out of the Naval Academy" | DISPUTED (age) | Sources give his age as 21, 22 or 23 and his birth year as 1839 or 1840, so the script no longer states an age. The 1912 account calls him "a boy in years" and a "beardless boy" (primary, [S1]). |
| 69 | The Virginia sits too deep to go up the river to Washington | UNKNOWN | Her draft of 23 feet is primary ([S1], her chief engineer). That this ruled out the Potomac is the usual account of Gideon Welles's reply to Stanton, but it was not confirmed in a source this time. |
| 95-96 | The speaking tube fails; orders are carried by the paymaster and a clerk | UNKNOWN | This is the standard account from Greene's 1885 article "In the Monitor Turret". The article itself could not be opened; one secondary page only says the tube "didn't work well" ([S9]). |
| 88 | "Later, the same guns safely fire double" | DISPUTED (meaning) | The 15-pound limit and later 30-pound charges are primary ([S1] introduction). Whether full charges would have pierced the Virginia is argued both ways ([S8]); the script only says "you'll never know". |
| 44 | "Worst day until Pearl Harbor" | WELL-DOCUMENTED as a description | Used by the American Battlefield Trust ([S4]); it is a characterisation, not a statistic. |
| 3 | "More than two hundred sailors" killed | WELL-DOCUMENTED, numbers vary | Cumberland 121 to 123, Congress about 120; totals of 240 to 261 killed are quoted ([S3], [S4]). The script stays below the lowest total. |
| 20 | "More than half an hour to turn around" | WELL-DOCUMENTED, numbers vary | Sources give 30 to 45 minutes ([S7]). |
| 119 | The Times: 149 first-class warships, now two | WELL-DOCUMENTED (secondary) | Quoted in a period history and by historyofwar.org, which calls it "something of an exaggeration" ([S10]); scene 120 says so. The 1862 issue itself was not opened. |

## Removed from the first version because they could not be confirmed

- Greene's exact age (22), the crew of 58, the gun counts of the Congress and the Cumberland.
- "Nobody has died today" on 9 March.
- "Then I can die happy".
- Worden standing in the turret when he was wounded (he was in the pilot house); the Washington scene on 8 March (the meeting was on 9 March).
